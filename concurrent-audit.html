What Is Concurrent Audit?

Concurrent audit is an examination performed contemporaneously with transactions, or as near to the transaction as possible. RBI describes it as an important early-warning mechanism for timely detection of irregularities and lapses.

How it helps an organisation

  • Early detection: Exceptions can be identified before they become larger control, operational or financial problems.
  • Stronger controls: Transactions can be checked against approved policies, delegated authority and applicable instructions.
  • Fraud-risk reduction: RBI's framework specifically highlights fraud-prone areas such as cash, deposits, advances, forex, off-balance-sheet items, cards and internet banking.
  • Faster escalation: Serious irregularities can be reported promptly to the appropriate management level for action.
  • Management assurance: Findings provide management and the Audit Committee with structured feedback on the operating control environment.

Think of it as an early-warning system

The objective is not simply to find mistakes after the period has ended. The value comes from shortening the gap between a transaction and its independent examination.

Important: Concurrent audit does not mean the auditor takes over management's decisions. The review checks whether transactions and decisions operate within the applicable policy, regulatory and delegated-authority framework.

Who Is It For?

Who Benefits From Concurrent Audit?

Concurrent audit is primarily relevant to regulated or high-risk financial operations rather than ordinary personal finances.

Banks & Financial Institutions

Particularly useful for high-risk branches, specialised branches, centralised processing units and activities such as treasury, forex, retail assets, corporate/SME operations and other areas identified for concurrent review.

Strong relevance

NBFCs & Other Regulated Entities

Audit design should be aligned with the entity's applicable RBI framework, risk profile and internal audit architecture. RBI's RBIA framework applies to specified NBFC categories and focuses on governance, risk management and controls.

Scope depends on entity

Individuals

Generally not a personal compliance requirement. An individual normally needs tax, accounting or financial-planning services instead. Concurrent audit becomes relevant only where the person operates or controls an organisation whose activities require this type of assurance.

Usually not applicable
Business Use Case

When Is It Useful For a Business?

The strongest business case exists where transaction volume, regulatory exposure, operational complexity or fraud risk makes retrospective checking too slow.

Situations where it adds value

  • Large transaction volumes or multiple operational locations.
  • High-risk branches, departments or products.
  • Loan processing, cash operations, deposits and other sensitive financial activities.
  • Centralised processing units where large-scale operations create concentrated risk.
  • Operations requiring prompt exception reporting and rectification.
RBI reference point:
The 2015 revised RBI guidelines for scheduled commercial banks (other than RRBs) state that concurrent audit at branches should cover at least 50% of advances and 50% of deposits, and identify high-risk and specialised operations for possible coverage.

What it is not

It is not a replacement for every other audit. Statutory audit, internal audit, risk-based internal audit, compliance reviews and other assurance functions can have different objectives and scopes.

It is also not a guarantee that fraud or error can never occur. Its value is in improving the probability of timely identification and escalation.

For Individuals

Is Concurrent Audit Useful For You Personally?

For a salaried person, freelancer, small individual investor or ordinary household, concurrent audit is generally not the appropriate service.

You may need other services

  • Income-tax return filing
  • Tax planning and compliance
  • Personal financial documentation
  • Investment or loan documentation support

Concurrent audit is usually not for

  • Routine personal banking
  • Normal salary income
  • Ordinary household expenses
  • Personal tax filing by itself
Risk & Consequences

What Can Happen If You Do Not Do It?

The consequence depends on whether concurrent audit is required by regulation, the institution's approved policy, or the engagement framework. It is not correct to say that every business automatically faces a statutory penalty simply for not having a concurrent audit.

Delayed Detection

Control failures, unusual transactions or documentation gaps may remain unnoticed for longer, increasing the cost and difficulty of correction.

Higher Fraud & Operational Risk

Where high-risk transactions are not independently reviewed promptly, opportunities for irregularities or fraudulent manipulation may be harder to detect early.

Regulatory / Audit Concerns

If an applicable regulatory requirement or approved institutional policy requires concurrent audit, failure to implement or properly follow it can create compliance and governance concerns and may lead to adverse audit observations or escalation.

Do not confuse risk with automatic penalty: RBI's concurrent-audit framework is entity- and scope-specific. The exact consequence of not conducting an audit must be assessed against the applicable RBI directions, the entity's category, Board-approved policy and engagement terms.
Typical Scope

What Can Be Checked?

The exact checklist is determined by the appointing institution and its approved audit scope. RBI's commercial-bank framework provides examples of key areas.

Cash & Banking

Cash transactions, cash verification, remittances and related controls.

Advances & Loans

Loan documentation and transaction checks where included in scope.

Deposits

Deposit-related transactions, controls and compliance checks.

Forex & Treasury

Specialised financial activities where applicable.

Cards & Digital Operations

Credit-card and internet-banking related risk areas where covered.

Regulatory & Internal Compliance

Checks against applicable internal procedures, delegated authority and regulatory instructions.

How It Works

Concurrent Audit Process

A practical engagement normally moves from scope definition to review, exception reporting, rectification and follow-up.

01

Define Scope

Identify locations, products, risk areas, records and applicable instructions.

02

Review Transactions

Examine transactions as they occur or as close to occurrence as practicable.

03

Identify Exceptions

Document deviations, control gaps and unusual or high-risk observations.

04

Escalate & Rectify

Minor issues may be corrected promptly; serious matters should be escalated according to the approved reporting system.

05

Follow Up

Track management action and ensure important observations are closed or appropriately reported.
